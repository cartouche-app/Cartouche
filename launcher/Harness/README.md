# Launcher harness

The launcher's own code, all of [`launcher/Sources`](../Sources) but the root view, built into a small app of the iOS Simulator on top of LiveContainer, where its tests do what a player does: install a game from an IPA, from Files, from another app or from the games folder, replace it with another build, start it, behind the console or in Cartouche's place, pause it, quit it, have iOS stop it, copy it to the games folder, and uninstall it, with Cartouche's setup, its telemetry and the saves along the way, and reset Cartouche itself. Each test then checks what the launcher and LiveContainer left: the files, the preferences, the data folder, what the console shows, what the player reads.

No real game is involved, nor any file of one: the IPAs are written by the tests, byte for byte, as nxnative and zip make them, with their `romfs.img` or with the `romfs.id` of a game built with its files apart ([`Tests/HarnessIPA.swift`](Tests/HarnessIPA.swift)).

## What is real, and what is stood in

Real, as Cartouche builds them:

- every file of `launcher/Sources` but `CartoucheRootView.swift`, compiled with `CARTOUCHE_HARNESS`;
- LiveContainer's `LCAppModel.swift`, `LCContainer.swift`, its unpacker (`unarchive.m`, on the iPhone's libarchive) and its texts (`Localizable.xcstrings`), which [`harness.sh`](harness.sh) copies from `app/LiveContainer` with every patch of [`app/patches/livecontainer`](../../app/patches/livecontainer) applied, as `app/scripts/prepare.sh` does;
- the catalogue, as `package.sh` copies it into the app;
- how the game's process asks for the keyboard, which brings it the controllers ([`CartoucheGuestControllers.m`](../LiveProcessSupport/CartoucheGuestControllers.m), [decision 123](../../docs/decisions/123.md)), run in the harness's own scene, in windows of the tests', as the game's scene would activate and resign active: the harness has no process of the game's.

Stood in, in [`Sources/`](Sources):

- LiveContainer's Objective-C, in Swift, line for line where the launcher reads or writes what it leaves: `LCAppInfo` with its `Info.plist` and `LCAppInfo.plist` and the steps of `patchExecAndSignIfNeed` ([`HarnessAppInfo.swift`](Sources/HarnessAppInfo.swift)), its folders, its list of apps as it reads it at launch, its preferences and where it keeps its certificate ([`HarnessLiveContainer.swift`](Sources/HarnessLiveContainer.swift));
- what the Simulator cannot do, which the harness does its own way and records: zsign's signature, of the files Cartouche lists for it (`HarnessLC.signedFiles`, [patch 0013](../../app/patches/livecontainer/0013-sign-only-the-files-ios-loads-code-from.patch)), which fails, as zsign does, on a file it cannot sign, a Mach-O of Apple's GPU, and whether iOS accepts the certificate; a start in Cartouche's place, which quits the app (`HarnessLC.launches`); the game's process behind the console, whose `AppSceneViewController` goes through LiveContainer's steps on its threads, may be ended by LiveProcess on an error, kept for Cartouche as [patch 0014](../../app/patches/livecontainer/0014-say-why-an-apps-process-ended-on-an-error-as-it-ends.patch) keeps it, writes the game's memory where Cartouche reads it and posts a controller's Home button, opens the game's scene on a TV, and which iOS may kill ([`HarnessScenes.swift`](Sources/HarnessScenes.swift));
- the network: every request of the app goes to [`HarnessNetwork.swift`](Sources/HarnessNetwork.swift), which records it and answers as each test says, or not at all, as without network. The [`Info.plist`](Info.plist) points Cartouche's servers at addresses of the harness's own;
- the built-in SideStore's renewal, which LiveContainer's bridge `CartoucheRefresh` hands it, and the record of Cartouche's signature SideStore keeps for its widget, where Cartouche reads its expiry: the renewal takes as long as a test says, then records a new expiry, or fails with SideStore's words, or never answers until the test lets it, or, LiveProcess taken out of the app, fails at once with LiveContainer's ([`HarnessSideStore.swift`](Sources/HarnessSideStore.swift)); and LocalDevVPN, connected unless a test says otherwise;
- the iPhone: one whose GPU reads BC-compressed textures and whose signature carries Increased Memory Limit, with no TV, unless a test says otherwise. A test hands Cartouche a TV as iOS does, a window with Cartouche's TV screen in the harness's own scene; it says that iOS captures the iPhone's screen, as iOS 27 does to copy it to a TV it has not handed Cartouche or to record it, with the capture state of that scene, and where the sound goes;
- on an iPad, the way the game's screen is turned and the way the iPad is held: the Simulator turns only from its menus, so a test sets both, and the log says what iPadOS made of the screen's request to keep its way.
- VoiceOver and Switch Control, which keep the Home button over a game on screen: off, unless a test says they run.

The knobs a test sets are in `HarnessLC` (signing, the certificate, the game's process, the GPU, the sound on a TV, the iPad's turns, VoiceOver, LocalDevVPN and whether Cartouche is in front), and are reset before each test, with LiveContainer's folders, the app group and the launcher's own state ([`Tests/HarnessCase.swift`](Tests/HarnessCase.swift)). The main thread must never be held more than 1.5 s, where the console draws: a watchdog times each probe until the main queue starts it. If the wait was too long, the watchdog fails the test and says what the launcher was doing as the wait began and ended, and where the main thread was, all its frames, read from another thread up to ten times a second, as often as the Mac lets it, from a fifth of a second into the wait. The time the main thread waits for RunningBoard's answer does not count: iOS's daemon that grants an app's assertions, its time in the background among them, answers an iPhone at once, and a busy Mac of the CI after seconds, 1.88 s once as Cartouche asked for its time in the background at the start of an install, 4.17 s once as UIKit told the keyboard's process of a new key window, which failed a test on nothing the launcher does ([`Tests/WatchdogTests.swift`](Tests/WatchdogTests.swift)); the log says such a wait of a second or more, with its frames. Before the first test, the harness has iOS make a bookmark of a folder once: the simulator's first one waits on a file provider starting up, which an iPhone did long before the player picks a folder, and the watchdog watches Cartouche, not the simulator. For the same reason the app writes its standard error to a file of its own ([`Sources/HarnessStandardError.swift`](Sources/HarnessStandardError.swift)), as an app the player starts writes it to `/dev/null`: as Xcode runs an app, os_log mirrors every line, NSLog's included, to standard error, which Xcode reads, and a line logged from the main thread waited there 19.8 s once, at the start of a run on a busy Mac of the CI, until Xcode read it ([`Tests/LoggingTests.swift`](Tests/LoggingTests.swift)). Each test also has Cartouche watch the controllers before the watchdog starts, as its console does from the moment it opens, long before the player starts a game: the harness has no console, and the first game a run started loaded GameController on the main thread instead, 1.62 s once on a busy Mac of the CI. Before the first controller test, a game's window asks for the keyboard and a text field of the game's takes it, once: a process's first keyboard, and its first text field typed in, load what UIKit types with and what shows a selection, the blur behind its cursor among them, 1.51 s once on a busy Mac of the CI, which on an iPhone happens in the game's process, never in Cartouche's. A test that takes more than 20 seconds says in the log where its time went: setting up, waiting for what the test before left, its longest wait and what for, cleaning up.

## The tests

- [`InstallTests`](Tests/InstallTests.swift): an install from an IPA, step by step, a game installed again over another build with its saves and settings, uninstalled with or without its saves, an app that is no game, signing that fails or leaves files out, with its reason, the kind of file and how many for the measurements, a game that bundles a Metal pipeline archive of Apple's GPU, as Breath of the Wild does, signed without it, an encrypted app, IPAs that are no IPA, cut short or damaged, without an app or without `Payload`, an app whose `Info.plist` cannot be read or gives no bundle identifier, and the question before an install on a GPU nxnative refuses.
- [`PlayTests`](Tests/PlayTests.swift): a start in Cartouche's place: the data folder, nxnative's switch for its settings screen in `env.txt`, the game's language, a game signed with an old certificate, a certificate iOS no longer accepts, a game LiveContainer did not sign whole, signed again whole once at its start, or from the notice that says why.
- [`BehindConsoleTests`](Tests/BehindConsoleTests.swift): a game behind the console, the Home button, Quit, a game iOS stops, near its memory limit or without Increased Memory Limit, LiveProcess missing or refusing, which leaves nothing of the start there, a game LiveProcess ends on an error, with its words in the report, a signature iOS refused, signed again from the notice, the iPhone that may lock again as the console shows or kept awake by an install, another game in its place.
- [`UprightTests`](Tests/UprightTests.swift): which ways the screen of a game behind the console turns on an iPhone: upright too for a game that lays itself out upright, as nxnative's built since 4 November 2026, sideways only for one built before, after a pause too.
- [`IPadTests`](Tests/IPadTests.swift): the screen of a game behind the console on an iPad, which turns Cartouche's scenes as it is held, whatever the game allows: a game made to be played sideways there, as nxnative's built before 4 November 2026 are, stays sideways as the iPad tilts upright or lies flat, turns from one side to the other with the iPad, lets the console turn while it is paused, and asks the player to turn an iPad held upright sideways; a game that turns every way, as nxnative's built since do on an iPad, turns with the iPad; the launcher's words call the iPad the iPad; on an iPhone, which turns the screen itself, none of it.
- [`HomeButtonTests`](Tests/HomeButtonTests.swift): the Home button over a game behind the console, which shows as the game starts, fades away a few seconds after the game shows and stays where it was, so that a touch on its place brings it back and shows no console, and a touch on it then shows the console; which shows again as the game comes back from the console, says once how to bring it back, and stays over the cover that hides the game from a copy of the screen, and for VoiceOver.
- [`ControllerTests`](Tests/ControllerTests.swift): a game behind the console and its controllers: while the game's scene is active, its process asks for the keyboard, which brings them, without showing one, nothing on the game's screen, no touch taken from the game and nothing for VoiceOver, and lets it go as the scene resigns active, then asks again; a text field of the game's keeps the keyboard until it lets it go; the game's process asks in the window the game shows; and the journal of a player's report says once for each game that the controller's presses reach it, as the game pauses or ends.
- [`TVTests`](Tests/TVTests.swift): a TV while a game runs behind the console: the game's own scene there over its card, never the iPhone's screen, the game's screen sideways only while the game's picture is there, the card alone while the game is paused, a game without a scene for the TV, Cartouche in the background, the TV plugged in during the game and unplugged, the TV given back to iOS as the game ends, and the game hidden as soon as iOS copies the iPhone's screen, to a TV it has not handed Cartouche or to a recording, each said for what it is, unless the player shows it anyway; each step goes in the journal of a player's report, and so does, as the game pauses or ends, whether the game's process says the game's picture is on its scene there, with the nxnative it was built from.
- [`SetupTests`](Tests/SetupTests.swift): the built-in SideStore's certificate handed to LiveContainer, whether iOS accepts it, the Apple ID, the pairing file.
- [`RenewalTests`](Tests/RenewalTests.swift): Cartouche renewing itself as it comes to the front in the last three days of its signature, set up and with LocalDevVPN only, six hours after a renewal that failed, which says why, however often Cartouche comes back to the front meanwhile; never on a certificate Apple revoked, nor without LiveProcess in the app, where Cartouche says to install it again over itself; Renew now, one at a time; SideStore's other actions, which wait for a renewal under way, three minutes at most when it never ends.
- [`LocalDevVPNTests`](Tests/LocalDevVPNTests.swift): Cartouche having LocalDevVPN connect by itself, through the URL that opens Cartouche again, for Renew now, for the renewal it starts by itself, once in six hours at most, and for the sign-in, before SideStore is asked; going on after its limit when the tunnel never shows, counting only its time in front; nothing opened from the background; the guide's button, LocalDevVPN's page of the App Store when it is not on the iPhone.
- [`TelemetryTests`](Tests/TelemetryTests.swift): what leaves the iPhone, what each answer of the server does, the player's switch, which stops a report already on its way, erasing what was sent, Cartouche's own events, among them what renewed its signature and whether Cartouche renewed itself, and a game not signed whole reported by its reason, never its files.
- [`UnpackTests`](Tests/UnpackTests.swift): LiveContainer's unpacker and Cartouche's reader on the same IPAs, in Zip64 or not, stored or deflated, with links, and what each does with an IPA cut short or damaged.
- [`StorageTests`](Tests/StorageTests.swift): the games folder and the saves folder, as a folder of the iPhone: the games listed, a game installed from there, copied there and installed again, its saves sent after a game and back with its next install.
- [`SwitchSaveTests`](Tests/SwitchSaveTests.swift): a save the player exported on their Switch with JKSV, put in place of the game's save, before its first start too, its data folder made for it, the save it replaced put back, sent on to the saves folder as the newest copy, and none while the game runs.
- [`GameFilesApartTests`](Tests/GameFilesApartTests.swift): a game nxnative built with its files apart, `romfs.id` in its app and its `romfs.img` beside the IPA, installed from the games folder or from Files, without its image or with another, and a new build of it, which keeps the image the installed one holds, or needs another.
- [`ResetTests`](Tests/ResetTests.swift): System › Reset Cartouche on a Cartouche a player has used: what goes (the games and their data folders, those of the games uninstalled with their saves among them, the storage and its passwords, the Apple ID, the built-in SideStore's preferences, through cfprefsd, and files, Cartouche's preferences, its caches, journal and reports), what stays (the certificate, the pairing file, what Apple knows the iPhone by, Apple's limit on sign-ins while it counts, LiveContainer's preferences in the app group, the telemetry's number, the player's folders and what they hold, even in Cartouche's own space in Files), Cartouche started again on its console, nothing erased while a game plays or installs, and the saves here alone, named, sent to the saves folder first, and back from there with the game.
- [`LogShareTests`](Tests/LogShareTests.swift): a game's log, shared from its sheet: the logs nxnative writes, found in the data folder the game plays with once it has played, the zip of them as the game wrote them, and what Send to Cartouche sends of them, cleaned.
- [`LoggingTests`](Tests/LoggingTests.swift): the harness itself: what Cartouche logs never waits for Xcode to read it, its standard error being a file.
- [`WatchdogTests`](Tests/WatchdogTests.swift): the harness itself: the watchdog counts the main thread held and says where it was, all its frames, but not the time it waited for RunningBoard.

The storage servers themselves are tested against real ones by [`launcher/StorageTests`](../StorageTests/README.md), and the launcher's rules on their own by `swift test --package-path launcher`.

## Running it

On a Mac with Xcode and XcodeGen (`brew install xcodegen`), after `git submodule update --init app/LiveContainer`:

    launcher/Harness/harness.sh OUT                                     # every test
    launcher/Harness/harness.sh OUT CartoucheHarnessTests/InstallTests  # one class, or one test
    launcher/Harness/harness.sh --ipad OUT CartoucheHarnessTests/IPadTests  # on an iPad

It runs on an iPhone 17 Pro of the newest iOS of that Xcode, made if there is none; with `--ipad`, on a 13-inch iPad Pro (M5). `OUT` receives the results (`CartoucheHarness.xcresult`), Xcode's log (`test.log`), what the app wrote to its standard error (`console.txt`, the launcher's NSLog lines among them) and `failures.txt`, the tests that failed and why. The [Launcher harness](../../.github/workflows/harness.yml) workflow runs it on every pull request that changes the launcher's sources, the harness, the game's process's code for the controllers, LiveContainer or its patches, or the catalogue, once for what the pull request changes in them, and on main after each merge that changes them: every test on the iPhone and, at the same time on a Mac of their own, `IPadTests` and `BehindConsoleTests` on the iPad, whose results go in `OUT/ipad` (`launcher-harness-ipad`).

## Adding a test

A test subclasses `HarnessCase`, runs on the main actor, and waits with `waitUntil`, never with a sleep on the main thread. Make the IPAs it needs with `HarnessIPA` and `HarnessZip`, never from a real game. A fix of the launcher comes with the test that failed without it.
