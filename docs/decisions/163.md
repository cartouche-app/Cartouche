# 163. Build folders chosen on a NAS keep the games' build folders between two builds: the games are built on this Mac

2026-11-25

**Build folders**, in the Builder's settings, chosen on a NAS's share is where the Builder keeps the games' build folders, never where it builds them ([`shelf.py`](../../builder/cartouche_builder/shelf.py)). A game is built on this Mac, in the Builder's own folder (`~/Library/Application Support/Cartouche Builder/Builds`). Before its build, its build folder comes back from the NAS, when this Mac has none or an older one, with what its next build reuses: its shader caches, its app's build, its logs. Once built, or failed, it goes back to the NAS and this Mac's is removed, unless **Keep the extracted files** is on. What nxnative makes again in minutes, as big as the game, never travels: the game's extracted files and its converted packages.

On the NAS, a build folder is replaced whole: it is written beside the old one (`<title ID>_build.incoming`), which goes once the new one is there. A NAS that does not take it, or **Stop** as it goes, leaves the one before on the NAS and this Mac's for the next build, which then takes this Mac's, newer. Each folder kept has a note in the NAS's `.cartouche-builder/`, with when it was kept and its size by part. The Storage page reads that note instead of measuring the NAS's files again, and lists those folders apart, under **Build folders on your NAS**, with the NAS's room; **Cartouche keeps … on this Mac** counts this Mac's alone. A folder left on the NAS by an earlier version is measured once, then noted.

A folder is on a NAS when it is under a share the Builder knows (`shares` in `config.json`) or on a network filesystem as `mount` says (`smbfs`, `nfs`, `afpfs`, `webdav`), and the Builder mounts its share again before a batch, as it does the dumps folder and the games folder. A disk plugged into the Mac is no NAS: the games are built there, as before.

Not tried on a real Mac and NAS yet.

**Why.** On 25 November 2026, every build failed on a player's Mac whose build folders were on their NAS's SMB share: nxnative extracted the game there, then could not open the RomFS it had just written (`OSError: [Errno 9] Bad file descriptor`), or read it (`[Errno 5] Input/output error`). macOS's SMB client does not hold nxnative's writes and reads of the same files. The player had chosen the NAS to keep the build folders, and their caches, without keeping them all on the Mac's disk: "store the builds there once made, and fetch them the next time to take the cache, but no build straight on the NAS". Measuring those folders over SMB also took minutes for a large game (100 s for Mario Kart World's 43,064 files), which the Storage page did at each look.
