# 161. A documentation for players at docs.cartouche.cloud, and a shorter site

2026-11-25

Cartouche has a documentation for players, [`docs-site/`](../../docs-site/README.md), at <https://docs.cartouche.cloud/>: how to install Cartouche and get to a first game, every screen of Cartouche on the iPhone and the iPad, Cartouche for Mac (its console, the Builder and the iPhone paired with it), the Cartouche Installer and a player's own certificate, Cartouche Cloud and its page on the iPhone, troubleshooting, the questions people ask, where to get help, privacy and the legal notes, in English, French, Spanish and German, each page at the same path in each language, with the app's own words for its buttons and its screens from the CI's galleries.

- **Starlight**, Astro's theme for documentation, rather than Retype, which the owner gave as an example: it is free software with no page limit and no paid tier, it has the four languages and their fallback in one project, a search that runs in the reader's browser from the site's own files (Pagefind), and it builds static pages, which is what Cloudflare serves.
- **At Cloudflare**, as a Worker with static assets alone, `cartouche-docs`, in the account of the zone `cartouche.cloud`, at its domain only: no `workers.dev` or preview address, no logs, and the documentation's 404 page for an address it does not have. The [Documentation](../../.github/workflows/docs.yml) workflow builds it when `docs-site/` changes on main, runs its tests, then deploys it with wrangler and the token the source's Worker already uses, `CLOUDFLARE_DOMAIN_TOKEN` of the environment `cloudflare`, which may edit Workers and their routes: nothing new is created at Cloudflare beyond what that token allows, and Cloudflare makes the name's record and certificate itself. While the token may not deploy Workers, the workflow warns and says what to add to it. The CI builds and checks it on every pull request.
- **What it may say** is what the site may say (decision 40): no price of Cartouche Cloud, no amount of memory as an argument, no link to a game, a key or firmware, no image from inside a game, and nothing loaded from another site, which its Content Security Policy enforces. Its tests check every link and heading, the links out, each of these rules and each page in each language.

The showcase site, [`site/`](../../site/README.md), stays short and sends readers there for the details. Each section keeps its screens and says what Cartouche does in a sentence or two; its lists become chips that name each feature, and a link leads to the section's page in the documentation, in the reader's language. The header, the first screen and the footers link to the documentation, and Cartouche for Mac's guide is its page there. The FAQ keeps its questions on what Cartouche is, costs and sends; the others, on the iPad and the Mac, the TV, a player's own certificate and building games, are in the documentation. nxnative-nobc is named in *What you need*, still as a separate fork of nxnative by mianmuhammadse, with its link. About a third of the site's words go. `site/tests` admits the documentation's pages among its links and checks that each one is in the language of the text around it and is a page `docs-site/` has.

It amends [decision 40](../decisions.md) and [decision 160](160.md), whose site said each feature in full.

**Why.** On November 25, 2026, the owner found the site had too much in it, text above all, and asked for a lighter site beside a good documentation for players that explains and details every app and Cartouche Cloud, at Cloudflare if possible. A page that names what Cartouche does reads at a glance; a player who wants the steps finds them in the documentation, searchable, in their language.
