# 151. The install's screen says how a game comes and how fast, and what would go faster

2026-11-24

While a game installs from the player's storage, the install's screen says how fast it comes, in MB/s over the last eight seconds, and, from the player's Mac, how: **By cable**, **Through Tailscale**, or on the local network, with what the Mac's Cartouche Builder says of its side, **On the Wi-Fi, the Mac on 2.4 GHz** (or 5, or 6), or **the Mac on Ethernet** ([`CartoucheTransferSpeed.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheTransferSpeed.swift), [`CartoucheGameWorkView.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheGameWorkView.swift)). Under that line, in orange, what would go faster:

- the Mac on its 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, at once, before any speed is measured: plug the iPhone into the Mac with a cable ([#289](https://github.com/cartouche-app/Cartouche/pull/289), [decision 143](143.md)), or put the Mac on the 5 GHz Wi-Fi;
- slow on the network to the Mac, below 8 MB/s, a game of 20 GB taking over 40 minutes: the cable, or a 5 GHz Wi-Fi for both, the iPhone's own Wi-Fi being as likely the slow one;
- slow through Tailscale: the game goes over the internet, and at home the same Wi-Fi or the cable is much faster;
- slow from a NAS of the local network (SMB, FTP, SFTP): a 5 GHz Wi-Fi;
- nothing through the cable, the fastest, nor from a bucket, a Google Drive or Cartouche Cloud, which the internet brings.

A transfer said slow is no longer once above 12 MB/s, so that a speed about the limit does not take the advice off and on. The speed is measured on the bytes read ([`CartoucheResumingSource.onRead`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheResumingSource.swift)); the seconds the connection takes to come back, and the opening of the image of the game files, count in neither the speed nor the average.

The bridge's info tells the iPhone how its connection reaches the Mac (`network`): `cable`, `wifi` with the band of the Mac's Wi-Fi channel, which CoreWLAN reads without asking anything (only the network's name needs Location Services), `ethernet`, or `other` for Tailscale's tunnel or a VPN's ([`BridgeNetwork.swift`](../../builder/mac/Sources/BuilderKit/BridgeNetwork.swift)). Its words are text, so that a newer Mac's other words leave an older iPhone's answer readable; an older Cartouche Builder says nothing, and the iPhone then says **On the local network**. Cartouche asks it as an install connects to the Mac, and again when it connects again.

The Builder's iPhone page says the same of what it sends, as the rule that the Mac mirrors the iPhone asks ([decision 95](095.md)): under the iPhone reading a game, **Sending a game: 3.1 MB/s, on this Mac's 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi**, with the same advice ([`BridgeView.swift`](../../builder/mac/Sources/CartoucheBuilder/BridgeView.swift)). Cartouche's console on the Mac installs no game over a connection: it plays those built on the Mac.

`game.installed` and an install's `error` carry `speed`, the average in MB/s, and `macBand`, the band of the Mac's Wi-Fi, never the network's name nor an address ([telemetry/README.md](../../telemetry/README.md)).

Neither CoreWLAN's band nor the speeds have been tried on a real Mac and iPhone yet.

**Why.** A contributor's first installs from the Mac went very slowly or stopped, the Mac being on a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi; on the 5 GHz network they ran much faster, and a line on the install's screen saying which connection it used, or a warning on 2.4 GHz, would have saved an hour (2026-11-24). An iPhone cannot read the band of its own Wi-Fi without an entitlement a free account does not have, but the Mac can read its own: the Mac is the one that tells. The cable, since [decision 143](143.md), is the way that depends on no Wi-Fi.
