# 165. Cartouche for Mac renews Cartouche on the iPhone with a new profile, keeping the player's Apple session and never their password

2026-11-26

Cartouche for Mac carries a helper, `cartouche-signer` ([`builder/mac/signer`](../../builder/mac/signer/README.md)), that renews Cartouche on an iPhone plugged in to the Mac, or on its Wi-Fi with Wi-Fi sync on. It renews the way SideStore does on the iPhone: Apple makes a new provisioning profile for Cartouche's App ID, for this iPhone and the account's certificates, and the iPhone's `misagent` installs it. Nothing is signed or installed again. Cartouche keeps its games, its saves and its Increased Memory Limit, and no App ID is taken while Cartouche's still exists. The helper does this in Cartouche's last two days, or once it has expired, and never earlier unless the player asks. Before that window it does not reach Apple. A normal renewal is three requests: the account's teams, its App IDs, and the profile.

The profile is installed only if it opens Cartouche: Cartouche's App ID, this iPhone, a certificate of the profile Cartouche runs under now, and every entitlement that profile grants. An App ID that is gone is registered again with the app group and Increased Memory Limit the Installer gives it. An iPhone missing from the profile is registered. If the certificate Cartouche was signed with is gone from the account, no profile is installed and the helper says so: only an install over Cartouche opens it again (below). The helper never revokes a certificate, never touches a Cartouche installed with another Apple Account, and never resets the anisette machine on a 429 ([decision 63](../decisions.md)). After a renewal, it writes the renewal's date in Cartouche's Documents (`.cartouche/renewal.json`, the fields of SideStore's own record) for Cartouche to show.

The Mac signs in once, with the password and a two-factor code, as the Installer does. It then keeps only Apple's tokens, in a file in the Builder's folder that only the player's account can read (`Signer/session.json`): the Xcode token and the session it came from, which gives a new token without the password when the last one ends. The password goes to Apple and nowhere else. It is never written, never in the helper's arguments, and never in the keychain, which an ad hoc signed app would have to ask for again after each update. The Mac never asks for the password when it opens, nor to build or play. It asks only when a renewal is due and Apple has ended the session, discreetly, with a notification and never a window that takes over.

The first step was the engine and its tests: the helper in `Contents/Helpers`, and the app's side of it (`Signer.swift`). The second is what the player sees. Under **Renewing Cartouche**, on the Builder's **iPhone** page, whether the bridge is on or not, the player signs in once and sees each iPhone the Mac reaches. For each one it shows until when Cartouche opens, when this Mac renews it, or why it did not, in the player's words, with **Renew** ([`RenewalView.swift`](../../builder/mac/Sources/CartoucheBuilder/RenewalView.swift), [`SignerWords.swift`](../../builder/mac/Sources/BuilderKit/SignerWords.swift)). The app looks at the iPhones every half hour while it runs ([`Renewals.swift`](../../builder/mac/Sources/CartoucheBuilder/Renewals.swift)). It renews the copy its session's team signed, when it is due ([`SignerPlan.swift`](../../builder/mac/Sources/BuilderKit/SignerPlan.swift)). After a failure it tries again six hours later, or one hour later when the iPhone was away or locked. A notification without a sound asks for the password only when the player signed in on this Mac, Apple ended that session, and a renewal is due, at most once a day. Another says once when only an install opens a copy again. Nothing opens by itself. A player who never signed in reads it on the page alone. The helper's message, which can name the account or the iPhone, goes to `Signer/renewals.log` (`0600`), never to the page or to the measurements. The measurements are `builder.renewed`, `builder.renewFailed` and `builder.appleSignIn`. On the iPhone, `signature.renewed` has `via: mac` when the Mac's date in Cartouche's Documents is the latest: Cartouche reads it as it reads SideStore's ([`CartoucheRenewalRules.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheRenewalRules.swift)). The third step is that install over Cartouche, for a copy whose certificate is gone, revoked by another computer or app with the same Apple Account ([`reinstall.rs`](../../builder/mac/signer/src/reinstall.rs)). The helper installs the newest Cartouche its source lists, the one its built-in SideStore updates from, downloaded from the repository's releases on GitHub alone and checked against the source's size and SHA-256. It signs it as the Installer does, with isideload, for the session's team and with a certificate of this Mac's, *Cartouche for Mac*, which also goes in Cartouche's SideStore framework for Cartouche to renew itself with. The identifier stays the copy's, so iOS installs it over the copy and keeps its container: the games, the saves, Increased Memory Limit on its App ID, and SideStore's pairing file. It never revokes a certificate: an account with as many as Apple allows gets nothing installed, and the page says to free one in the Installer's **Certificates** page. The app does the install by itself once Cartouche has expired, when none of its games can be playing, and six hours after an install that failed. Before, the page shows **Reinstall**, with the install's progress, and a notification says once that it is needed. Another says when the Mac did it by itself. The measurements are `builder.reinstalled`, and `builder.renewFailed` with `how: install`. None of this has been tried on a real iPhone or Mac yet.

**Why.** On 26 November 2026, 76 of the 122 copies of Cartouche played before the last 30 days were gone, 38 of them probably expired. Half of those 38 (19) had a Mac among their storage, and 52 of the 122 were paired with a Mac. SideStore on the iPhone renews only through LocalDevVPN, and with the session of its last sign-in, which Apple ended 0 to 15 days after it (14 copies). The Mac reaches the iPhone without either. The player chose to keep the session without the password: "Oui sans garder le mot de passe, mais faut pas que ca lui demande son mot de passe a chaque fois qu'il utilise le builder non plus" (2026-11-26). A profile alone keeps what a reinstall would risk: the games, the saves, the memory limit and the App IDs of the week.
