# 167. A game's mods: its sheet adds them from a folder or a ZIP and turns them on in an order, and nxnative lays them over the game's files

2026-11-26

A game's sheet has **Mods**, on the iPhone, the iPad and in the console on the Mac ([`CartoucheModsView.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheModsView.swift)). **Add a mod** takes a mod from Files, or from the Mac, as its folder or its ZIP. Each mod shows its name, how many files it has and their size, a switch that turns it on or off, **Move up**, **Move down** and **Delete**. Where two mods change the same file, the one higher up wins. A mod just added goes to the top. The row on the sheet says how many are on. Nothing changes while the game runs behind the console, and a change takes effect at the game's next start. At most 16 are on at once.

A mod is a folder of files that the game reads in place of its own: textures, texts, music, levels. nxnative reads them itself, from the folders `DM_ROMFS_OVERLAY` names, in their order, before the game's own files ([`CartoucheMods.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheMods.swift)). Before each start, Cartouche writes that line in the game's `env.txt`, with the romfs folders of the mods turned on, by the paths the game sees, and takes it out when none is on. The line is Cartouche's own, as `DM_LAUNCHER` is, and every other line of the file stays.

**Where they live.** With the game, where it keeps its saves: on the iPhone, in the Documents of the game's data folder, `mods/<name>/romfs` and `mods/mods.json` (their names, their order, which are on, the game's version when each came), out of iCloud backups as the games are ([decision 153](153.md)); on the Mac, in nxnative's folder of the game, `~/Library/Application Support/nxnative/<title ID>/mods`, whose `env.txt` nxnative's launcher reads first. A game uninstalled without its saves loses its mods with them. A folder the player copies into `mods/` by hand shows too, turned off.

**What comes.** The romfs folder of what the player picks: at its root, under the mod's own folder, or under `atmosphere/contents/<title ID>/`, as on a Switch's microSD card. A pick that holds several romfs folders, as a pack of variants does, gives several mods, all turned off; a single mod comes turned on. Only the romfs folder is copied or unpacked, and a Mac's `__MACOSX`, `._` files and `.DS_Store` are left out. What does not come, with a sentence that says why: no romfs folder, an empty one, a mod of another game by the title ID in its path, a link or a path that leads out of the mod, and a mod that changes the game's code too, with files in an `exefs` folder beside its romfs. On the iPhone, iOS runs only the code signed with the game and nxnative leaves a mod's code plugin out, so the game would wait for it on a black screen. On the Mac, such a plugin must first be rewritten by nxnative's `prepare_mod.py`, which Cartouche does not do yet ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#later)).

**Another version.** Each mod keeps the game's version when it came. When the installed game is another version, its row says that a mod made for one version may not work with another. Nothing is refused for it.

**Another device.** Mods stay on the device they were added on. The saves folder takes only `save/` and `save_device/` ([`CartoucheSaveSync.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheSaveSync.swift)), and a mod can take gigabytes that the player already has where they found it. Bringing them along is for later ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#later)).

**Measured.** A session's `mods`, how many mods nxnative took, from the line it writes as it reads `env.txt` ([telemetry/README.md](../../telemetry/README.md)). Never a mod's name, folder or files, and the names of the mods' folders are taken out of a log sent to Cartouche (`<mod>`, [`CartoucheGameLogShare.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheGameLogShare.swift)). Cartouche downloads no mod for the player and names no site that offers them.

None of this has been tried on a real iPhone or Mac yet.

**Why.** On 26 November 2026, the owner chose, among ideas other front ends of nxnative already offer, mods for each game, its most important half first and the rest on the roadmap: mods that change a game's files, which nxnative lays over the game's on the iPhone and the Mac alike, with nothing to sign or rewrite. nxnative keeps the line in 4,096 bytes, the first folder that has a file winning (`hle_fs.c`): 16 mods, whose paths take one to two hundred bytes each on an iPhone, fit with room to spare. The paths are written again before each start because an iPhone's folders move when Cartouche is installed again. Mods that change the code are refused on the iPhone because nxnative cannot run them there, and told apart on the Mac because they need a step Cartouche does not take yet: a mod accepted that leaves the game on a black screen, or that a Mac game ignores, would read as Cartouche's fault.
