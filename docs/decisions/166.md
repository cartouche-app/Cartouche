# 166. A game that loses its last controller while the player plays pauses, and a controller that connects again brings it back

2026-11-26

A game played with a controller that has none for 3 seconds pauses, as the Home button pauses it. On the iPhone, a game behind the console ([decision 38](../decisions.md)) goes behind it, its scene in the background, and the console says that the controller disconnected, on the home and on the game's sheet. On the Mac, Cartouche's console comes in front of the game's app, which it hides, and stops the game's processes (`SIGSTOP`), and the console says the same. A controller that connects again brings the game back, as **Resume** does: on the Mac, its processes go on (`SIGCONT`) and its app comes back in front. **Resume** and **Play** bring it back too, and so does the game's app brought to the front from the Dock on the Mac. A link that drops for less than 3 seconds, or one controller of two that goes, pauses nothing.

A game played on the screen alone, which had no controller, never pauses for one. Neither does a game the player brought back with **Resume** and no controller: they play on the screen from then on, until a controller connects again, which counts from then. A game the player paused themselves, with the Home button, stays paused when a controller connects. **Pause when the controller disconnects**, under **While a game runs** on the System page, turns it off. It is on unless the player turns it off.

The rule is one type, apart from the controllers and the screens ([`CartoucheControllerLoss.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheControllerLoss.swift)), so that it is the same on the iPhone and on the Mac, and tested on its own. `CartoucheControllerPause` ([`CartoucheGamepad.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheGamepad.swift)) follows the controllers and the game, and asks it what to do. On the iPhone, the game's own process says whether it has controllers, as one connects or goes and as its scene activates ([`CartoucheGuest.m`](../../launcher/LiveProcessSupport/CartoucheGuest.m)). Its word wins over Cartouche's own, since iOS hands a controller to the process that has the keyboard ([decision 123](123.md)). On the Mac, nxnative's app takes no word from outside to pause, and plays on when it loses the focus. Its processes are stopped instead, as iOS suspends a game in the background, which nxnative's host already handles: its sound starts its count again rather than make up the time. A builder that starts again while a game is stopped finds it stopped and treats it as paused for its controller, so the game is never left stopped with nothing to say so.

Each pause goes in the journal of the player's reports. When it ends, it goes in the measurements, as `game.controllerPause` ([telemetry/README.md](../../telemetry/README.md)), with how it ended (`controller`, `resume`, `quit`, `ended`) and how long it lasted.

A game started in Cartouche's place, which is then Cartouche itself, does not pause for its controller yet. Doing it would take a pause of Cartouche's own drawn over the game, with nxnative's scene told that it goes to the background, from Cartouche's code inside the game. That is for later.

None of this has been tried on a real iPhone or Mac yet.

**Why.** A controller's battery that runs out, or its Bluetooth link that drops, left the game playing under the controls on the screen, and the race or the fight was lost before the player could reach the screen. Other front ends for nxnative pause a game whose controller goes. The player chose this one among the ideas taken from them, for the iPhone and the Mac (2026-11-26). Three seconds lets a link that drops for a moment, or a controller that wakes as a button is pressed, pause nothing.
