# 138. The site says what Cartouche Cloud does for a player with no Mac, and links to its site, never giving its price

2026-11-19

Cartouche's site, [`site/`](../../site/README.md), has a section on Cartouche Cloud, the service that builds a player's games on its Macs from the copy they send it, for a player with no Mac ([decision 136](136.md)). It says what the service does: the player sends their own copy from a computer, on its site, where their browser opens the game so that their keys never leave it; a Mac builds it with nxnative for them alone, and the copy is deleted once the game is built; Cartouche, linked to the player's account by a code, follows the build and installs the game built, which the service keeps two days. It says that a build that succeeds is paid for in credits bought in advance on the service's site, and that a build that fails costs nothing. Beside it, Cartouche's page of the service, rebuilt in HTML as the site's other screens are, shows the account's games past its credits. The header, the first lines, the install's fourth step, what one needs and the FAQ name it where they speak of building games, and the FAQ says what it keeps of a game, with a link to its privacy notice. What Cartouche does not do now says that it downloads the games Cartouche Cloud built for the player, as [LEGAL.md](../../LEGAL.md) says since 136. The README says the same in its own words.

The site links to Cartouche Cloud's first page, `https://build.cartouche.cloud/`, and to its privacy notice, and to nothing else of it. No page of the site gives a price: `site/tests` fails on an amount of money in what any of them says. It amends [decision 40](../decisions.md): the site links to Cartouche Cloud's site too.

**Why.** The owner wants Cartouche Cloud out for everyone, with the app, the site and an announcement (2026-11-18), and asked that the showcase site not mention its price (2026-11-18): the price stays on the service's own pages and in the app, which reads it from the service, so that it is said in one place. A player with no Mac learns from the site that they can still build their games, and where; the service's site says the rest, its price, its terms and how to get an account.
