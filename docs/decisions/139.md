# 139. The builder's fallback for a game's shader split goes back through every change of nxnative's splitter, with what the Mac learnt kept

2026-11-24

When nxnative's newest commit splits a game's shaders otherwise than its profile says (decision 133), every change of nxnative's splitter (`dm/gfx/tools/shader_split.py`) since the profile's tables last changed counts, not the three newest ([`fallback.py`](../../builder/cartouche_builder/fallback.py), `split_back`): the commit before each change, newest first, once for each splitter it holds, and none that holds the newest commit's. What the Mac learnt of the game's files is kept with them (`nxnative.json`, `split`): the commits whose split of them differed, the newest commits it was tried at among them, rule out every commit after them, which holds their splitter or a later one, unless the game built from these files at a newer commit since; a commit that failed them otherwise than by its split (its app build, an Xcode it was not tested with) rules out the commits before it, older still. Then (`split_order`):

- a game this Mac built from these files after the profile's tables, its IPA, its Mac app or the commit its split last built from, goes back to that commit, at most three newer ones tried first: its IPA stays as it is there, however many times the splitter changed since;
- a game this Mac never built goes back through the commits left newest first, the three newest one after the other, then each twice as far back as the one before, then the oldest: a few builds find a commit that splits it right, however many changes ago the splitter broke it, and the next time the game is built, the commits between that one and the newest that did not are tried, newest first.

A commit that fails otherwise ends the walk, but for the one this Mac built these files from, whose IPA stays; an Xcode an older commit was not tested with is such a failure, no longer one tried again at the next batch. This amends [decision 133](133.md).

**Why.** On 19 November 2026, nxnative changed its splitter twice in the morning: the commit before 8d62601f, which had built seven games on players' Macs (Final Fantasy Resonance, Fire Emblem: Three Houses, Triangle Strategy, Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity, Dark Souls: Remastered, Unicorn Overlord, Captain Toad), was the fourth change back, out of the fallback's reach, and the measurements show 30 failures at shader split on 9 Macs on the 19th and 20th, until nxnative's c9b12271 (21 November) read their shaders as their tables say again. The splitter changed 33 more times between 19 and 24 November: three changes are half a day of nxnative. Going back one change at a time through all of them could cost tens of builds; the doubling steps cost a few, and settle on an older commit than the newest that would build only until the game is built again. What the Mac learnt spares the builds that cannot succeed: the commits after one whose split differed split as it does or worse, unless nxnative fixed it since, which a build since shows.
