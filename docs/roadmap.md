# Roadmap

Cartouche is a launcher: it installs, starts and keeps the games the player builds with nxnative, and signs, renews and updates itself. Today the app does not build games ([decision 28](decisions.md)): Cartouche Builder, a program of its own, builds them on the player's Mac ([decision 29](decisions.md)). Building on the iPhone or on a Windows PC is planned ([decisions 55 and 60](decisions.md)). Since 0.13, Cartouche Builder plays the games on the Mac too, in Cartouche's console ([decision 95](decisions/095.md)), and Cartouche installs with no computer, signed on the iPhone with the player's own certificate, still to be tried with a real certificate ([decision 89](decisions/089.md)). Each release is checked on a real iPhone, not only in CI, and tested from a fresh install.

## Later

- **Building on the iPhone, or on a Windows PC.** A game built from the player's own files on the iPhone itself, with no Mac: Cartouche reads the game's package and the console's keys from Files or the player's own storage, runs nxnative's build on the phone and installs the game. What it takes: nxnative's code rewrite on iOS, which spike S2 ran on an iPhone in 0.1 with the very result of the Mac; the shaders, and the parts a Mac compiles with Xcode today; and room on the phone, in memory and in time, for the largest games ([decision 55](decisions.md)). The same pieces let Cartouche Builder build on a Windows PC too: the parts compiled with Xcode come ready-made, compiled on a Mac by the project with no game in them, since Apple's licence keeps its SDKs to Apple computers; the shaders are compiled with Apple's Metal tools for Windows, once nxnative's build takes a Metal compiler other than Xcode 27.2's; and a game that nxnative starts on a Mac during its build, to take its shaders out (Fire Emblem: Three Houses, the Unreal games), still needs a Mac ([decision 60](decisions.md)).
- **A complete Cartouche Builder for Windows.** Another way to the Windows PC above, asked for on November 18, 2026: Cartouche Builder doing on a Windows PC what it does on the Mac. It builds the player's games from their own files and builds them again when their tooling changes for them, in batches or at night, puts them in the games folder, and serves the iPhones paired with the PC their games and saves; only playing them stays on Apple's systems, where nxnative runs. It would follow a community fork of nxnative that builds on Windows, under the MIT licence and synced with nxnative every day, as Cartouche Builder follows nxnative today: its commits, its build script, what changed for each game. That fork's history has announced a Windows app since late October 2026: LLVM to compile, the iPhone's SDK 27.2 downloaded by the player, the shaders through SPIRV-Cross and Apple's Metal compiler for Windows, and an unsigned IPA; its documentation describes only the Mac so far. Nothing is tried, so a study comes first: what that fork builds on Windows, and for which games, those nxnative starts on a Mac during their build staying on the Mac; Apple's licence, which allows the iOS SDK only on Apple computers ([decision 60](decisions.md)), so where a PC would take it from, the question that can stop it all; shaders from a Metal compiler other than Xcode 27.2's, which nxnative refuses for NVN games unless forced; and the app itself: the builder's engine needs only Python's standard library and could run on Windows, but its window, made for the Mac, would be made again, as the Cartouche Installer's already is for macOS and Windows.
- **Games for the iPhones whose GPU reads no BC texture, built by Cartouche Builder.** Asked for on November 18, 2026: an iPhone with an A16 chip or older, such as an iPhone 15, whose GPU cannot read the BC-compressed textures the games use, plays a game drawn with NVN when its build converts them, as the `no-bc` branch of [mianmuhammadse/nxnative](https://github.com/mianmuhammadse/nxnative/tree/no-bc) does, and Cartouche tells such a build from its executable ([decision 137](decisions/137.md)). Cartouche Builder would build the player's games with that conversion for those iPhones, from that fork, or from nxnative should it take the conversion in. What it takes: building from another repository than nxnative's, with commits and breakages of its own; the games drawn with Vulkan or OpenGL, which the conversion does not cover; and the memory a converted game takes on those iPhones, more than its sheet says, which the sessions of converted builds will measure.
- **Cartouche Cloud building from nxnative-nobc for a linked iPhone without BC.** Asked for on November 26, 2026 ([issue #335](https://github.com/cartouche-app/Cartouche/issues/335)): Cartouche Cloud builds a player's games from nxnative, so a game it builds for an iPhone whose GPU reads no BC texture (iPhone 11 to 15) does not draw on it. It would build them from nxnative-nobc for such an iPhone, as Cartouche for Mac does since 0.8.0 ([decision 150](decisions/150.md)), and again when that fork changes. What it takes: the build knowing the model of each iPhone linked to the account, and the build machines fetching nxnative-nobc beside nxnative. Later, as chosen on November 24, 2026: the Builder and the iPhone first, Cartouche Cloud after.
- **The iPhone as a controller while playing on the Mac.** Asked for on November 24, 2026: a game played on the Mac, with the iPhone in hand as its controller. The iPhone would show a touch controller and send it over Cartouche's link to the Mac (Wi-Fi, Tailscale or the USB cable), and Cartouche on the Mac would hand it to the game. macOS lets an app make a virtual controller only with an entitlement Apple reserves for paid developer accounts, and an iPhone app cannot act as a Bluetooth controller, so the game has to read this input itself: nxnative's [issue #2152](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/2152) proposes it (`DM_NETPAD`, a pad per phone sent to the Mac's own loopback address), with no answer yet. Motion, for aiming with the iPhone's gyroscope, and rumble would come on top. Nothing is built before nxnative answers, and nothing is tried; the delay it adds is estimated at 1 to 2 ms by cable and 3 to 10 ms over Wi-Fi.
- **Mods that change a game's code, on the Mac, and mods on another device.** Since the version after 0.16.2, a game's sheet adds mods that change its files, on the iPhone, the iPad and the Mac, and refuses those that change its code too, with an `exefs` folder ([decision 167](decisions/167.md)). On the Mac, nxnative runs such a mod once its plugin is rewritten by its `scripts/prepare_mod.py`, into the mod's `exefs/nxnative/`: Cartouche would run that step as the mod comes, or say why it cannot, and the mod would then be turned on as the others are. On an iPhone, iOS runs only the code signed with the game, so they stay refused there. Mods stay on the device they were added on, as a mod can take gigabytes: they could follow a game to the player's other devices through their storage, as its saves do, or come again from a folder of mods there.

## Not planned

- Hosting, downloading or listing downloadable games, keys or firmware ([decision 1](decisions.md)).
- **An Apple TV version.** Games reach the TV from the iPhone instead: by AirPlay to an Apple TV, or by cable, a game shows there alone and is played with a controller paired to the iPhone ([decision 85](decisions.md)). An Apple TV version would need games built for tvOS, which nxnative does not build; an installer that renews them on the Apple TV, where neither SideStore nor LiveContainer runs, so a computer paired over the network would have to renew each game every seven days, three games at most with a free Apple account; room for games that often take more than 10 GB, where tvOS keeps only 500 KB of an app's data for sure and may delete the rest when space runs low; and Increased Memory Limit, which tvOS does not have ([decision 106](decisions.md)).
