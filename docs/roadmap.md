# Roadmap

Cartouche is a launcher: it installs, starts and keeps the games the player builds with nxnative, and signs, renews and updates itself. Today the app does not build games ([decision 28](decisions.md)): Cartouche Builder, a program of its own, builds them on the player's Mac ([decision 29](decisions.md)). Building on the iPhone or on a Windows PC is planned ([decisions 55 and 60](decisions.md)). Since 0.13, Cartouche Builder plays the games on the Mac too, in Cartouche's console ([decision 95](decisions/095.md)), and Cartouche installs with no computer, signed on the iPhone with the player's own certificate, still to be tried with a real certificate ([decision 89](decisions/089.md)). Each release is checked on a real iPhone, not only in CI, and tested from a fresh install.

## Later

- **Building on the iPhone, or on a Windows PC.** A game built from the player's own files on the iPhone itself, with no Mac: Cartouche reads the game's package and the console's keys from Files or the player's own storage, runs nxnative's build on the phone and installs the game. What it takes: nxnative's code rewrite on iOS, which spike S2 ran on an iPhone in 0.1 with the very result of the Mac; the shaders, and the parts a Mac compiles with Xcode today; and room on the phone, in memory and in time, for the largest games ([decision 55](decisions.md)). The same pieces let Cartouche Builder build on a Windows PC too: the parts compiled with Xcode come ready-made, compiled on a Mac by the project with no game in them, since Apple's licence keeps its SDKs to Apple computers; the shaders are compiled with Apple's Metal tools for Windows, once nxnative's build takes a Metal compiler other than Xcode 27.2's; and a game that nxnative starts on a Mac during its build, to take its shaders out (Fire Emblem: Three Houses, the Unreal games), still needs a Mac ([decision 60](decisions.md)).
- **A complete Cartouche Builder for Windows, with NX2Apple.** Another way to the Windows PC above, asked for on November 18, 2026: Cartouche Builder doing on a Windows PC what it does on the Mac. It builds the player's games from their own files and builds them again when their tooling changes for them, in batches or at night, puts them in the games folder, and serves the iPhones paired with the PC their games and saves; only playing them stays on Apple's systems, where nxnative runs. It would follow [NX2Apple](https://github.com/LNPANDY/NX2Apple), a community fork of nxnative under the MIT licence, synced with nxnative every day, as Cartouche Builder follows nxnative today: its commits, its build script, what changed for each game. Its history has announced a Windows app since late October 2026: LLVM to compile, the iPhone's SDK 27.2 downloaded by the player, the shaders through SPIRV-Cross and Apple's Metal compiler for Windows, and an unsigned IPA; its documentation describes only the Mac so far. Nothing is tried, so a study comes first: what NX2Apple builds on Windows, and for which games, those nxnative starts on a Mac during their build staying on the Mac; Apple's licence, which allows the iOS SDK only on Apple computers ([decision 60](decisions.md)), so where a PC would take it from, the question that can stop it all; shaders from a Metal compiler other than Xcode 27.2's, which nxnative refuses for NVN games unless forced; and the app itself: the builder's engine needs only Python's standard library and could run on Windows, but its window, made for the Mac, would be made again, as the Cartouche Installer's already is for macOS and Windows.
- **Installing Cartouche with no computer, from a SideStore the player already has.** An IPA of Cartouche that the player's own SideStore installs with their free Apple ID; at its first start, Cartouche's built-in SideStore takes that SideStore's certificate and pairing file through `sidestore://certificate` and `sidestore://pairing`, as [patch 0003](../app/patches/sidestore/0003-import-the-certificate-and-pairing-file-of-another-sidestore.patch) already does, each confirmed by the player, for a certificate with its private key, of the team that signed Cartouche. It is not supported today and has never been tried, so a study comes first: whether games start; how many of a free Apple ID's App IDs it takes, ten new ones in seven days, since that SideStore gives one to Cartouche and one to each of its two extensions, LiveProcess included; Increased Memory Limit, which SideStore does not request on a free account ([architecture](architecture.md#memory)), and whether the built-in SideStore brings it back by signing Cartouche again ([decision 105](decisions.md)); and which SideStore renews Cartouche, since the built-in one renews Cartouche alone ([decision 64](decisions.md)) while the player's would renew it too.

## Not planned

- Hosting, downloading or listing downloadable games, keys or firmware ([decision 1](decisions.md)).
- **An Apple TV version.** Games reach the TV from the iPhone instead: by AirPlay to an Apple TV, or by cable, a game shows there alone and is played with a controller paired to the iPhone ([decision 85](decisions.md)). An Apple TV version would need games built for tvOS, which nxnative does not build; an installer that renews them on the Apple TV, where neither SideStore nor LiveContainer runs, so a computer paired over the network would have to renew each game every seven days, three games at most with a free Apple account; room for games that often take more than 10 GB, where tvOS keeps only 500 KB of an app's data for sure and may delete the rest when space runs low; and Increased Memory Limit, which tvOS does not have ([decision 106](decisions.md)).
