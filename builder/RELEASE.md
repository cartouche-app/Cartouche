Cartouche Builder 0.6.0 plays your games on this Mac with your iPhone's saves, opens on Cartouche's console, and builds what Cartouche on your iPhone asks.

- **Cartouche's console on the Mac.** The window opens on Cartouche's console, the iPhone's own screens: your games for this Mac with their covers, the sheet of the game in focus beside them, **Play**, **Game settings**, **Quit** and **Remove from this Mac**. **Console** and **Builder**, at the top of the window, switch between the console and the builder's pages (⌘1, ⌘2), and the menu bar opens either. A game your NAS keeps that is not on this Mac yet is there too, with **Install from the NAS**. A game's page says its newest saves came from the saves folder, without naming a device.
- **Your games on this Mac.** Each game is now built for this Mac too: after its IPA, from the same nxnative commit and the same files, the builder keeps the game's app for this Mac in your games folder, beside its IPA. On a NAS it is kept there in one file, and installed on this Mac the first time you play the game here (in your Applications folder, or a folder you choose), then updated with each new build; on this Mac's own disk it is played from the games folder. **Play on this Mac**, on a game's sheet, in the library or in the menu bar, starts it with your iPhone's saves: the newest copy in your saves folder comes in before the game starts, and the Mac's saves go back after it, for Cartouche on your iPhone to take. Building for the iPhone or for this Mac can be turned off, for every game in **Settings › Builds** or for one game on its sheet, and the switches move as soon as you flip them, even with your games on a NAS. **Storage › Games on this Mac** lists the apps on this Mac, and puts one in the Trash; a game installed from a NAS stays there.
- **A game fills the screen.** A game you start from Cartouche fills the screen, as on a console, Cartouche behind it, and Cartouche comes back when you quit the game. ⌃⌘F puts the game in a window.
- **Your iPhone's saves folder, found.** Until you choose a saves folder, your Mac takes Cartouche's saves folder it finds on a share mounted here, your iPhone's on your NAS: your games start on the Mac from your iPhone's saves.
- **Builds from the iPhone.** Cartouche on a paired iPhone shows each game's build on this Mac and asks this Mac to build it, stop it or take it out of the queue; its System page does the rest of what the Builds page does: **Build what is due**, **Stop all**, **Follow**, and the nightly build. A game no Mac has built is never built from the iPhone, which cannot answer this Mac's question first: the iPhone says why, and **Build what is due** from it leaves the game aside, as the nightly build does.
- **What stops a build, said before it starts.** An Xcode older than the one nxnative builds its shaders with stops a build before its first game, and **This Mac's tools** offers **Update Xcode**. When nxnative refuses this Mac's Metal compiler, the game says so with both versions and is tried again at the next build, instead of failing as damaged files.
- **Room.** When the disk of the build folders has less room than **Keep at least … GB free**, the library says before a build how many gigabytes are missing and what makes room, and a game of a batch that ended, built or not, leaves its build folder to the games after it.
- **Games no Mac has built.** A game that at least three Macs tried in the last four weeks without any building it says so on its sheet, and building it asks first; the nightly builds leave it aside until this Mac has built it once. The list comes from the weekly nxnative page, and nothing new is sent for it.
- **An IPA another tool made.** An IPA in your games folder that another tool made, and that the builder cannot read, no longer stops the library: it is passed over.
- **From 0.5.0 or older**: your settings, your games and their builds stay. 0.5.0 and older do not find this version by themselves, since Cartouche's repository moved to its own organization on GitHub: download it once, and it updates itself again after that. Each game you already built is then due for this Mac, and the nightly build, if it is on, builds them all the next night. On a NAS, a game's Mac app takes about as much room as its IPA; to build for the iPhone alone, turn **This Mac** off in **Settings › Builds › Built for**.

Download `Cartouche-Builder-0.6.0.dmg`, for a Mac with Apple silicon (nxnative builds on macOS 26.6 or later), open it and drag **Cartouche Builder** onto **Applications**. The app is not notarized: the first time, open **System Settings › Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**. Its updates then open without asking.

Your games are yours: the builder builds the dumps of your own Switch, and shares nothing. How it works: [builder/README.md](https://github.com/cartouche-app/Cartouche/blob/main/builder/README.md).
